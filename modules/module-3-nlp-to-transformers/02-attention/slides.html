The problemMeaning depends on the right neighbors

The same word can mean very different things depending on the company it keeps. To understand any word, the model must look at the few neighbors that pin it down, however far away they sit.

Think of it asReading a sentence and, for each word, deciding which other words to glance back at. Attention is exactly that glance, made mathematical.

Query, key, valueA soft lookup in a table

  • Query (Q): what am I looking for?
  • Key (K): what does each entry advertise?
  • Value (V): the content each entry carries.

Picture a table, like a Python dict or a database. The query is compared to every key. Instead of one exact hit, you take a weighted blend of the values, more from the keys that match best.

Another viewA meeting: you weight each person's notes (values) by how well their expertise (key) answers your question (query).

Query, key, valueWhere Q, K, and V come from

The three vectors are not handed to the model. Each token's embedding \( x \) (from class 3.1, Text as data) is multiplied by three learned weight matrices.

$$ q = x W_Q, \quad k = x W_K, \quad v = x W_V $$

  • \( W_Q, W_K, W_V \) are learned during training, like any weights from class 2.4 (PyTorch fundamentals).
  • The same embedding, seen three ways: what to look for, what to advertise, what to share.

Query, key, valueNot a dictionary lookup

A dictionary returns one exact match. Attention is soft: a token blends a little of every other token, weighted by how well each key matches the query.

Soft, not exactThink of a fuzzy database lookup where the match is by similarity, and you keep a weighted mixture of every row rather than a single hit.

The formulaAttention, in one line

$$ \text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q K^{\top}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) V $$

Read it aloudScore every token against every token, turn the scores into percentages, then blend everyone's information by those percentages. The next four slides take it one piece at a time.

Decoded · 1 of 4Score: Q times K transpose

  • \( Q, K, V \) are the stacks of query, key, and value vectors for all tokens.
  • \( Q K^{\top} \) takes the dot product of each query with every key.
  • A dot product is a similarity score, so this gives, for each token, a relevance score against every other token.

Decoded · 2 of 4Scale: divide by \( \sqrt{d_k} \)

\( d_k \) is the length of a key vector. Dividing by \( \sqrt{d_k} \) keeps the scores from growing large when the vectors are long.

Just a stabilizerLong vectors produce big dot products. Without the divide, softmax turns into a near one-hot spike that starves every other token of gradient. The \( \sqrt{d_k} \) is a normalizer, nothing more.

Decoded · 3 of 4Softmax: scores into percentages

$$ \text{softmax}(s)_j = \frac{e^{s_j}}{\sum_k e^{s_k}} $$

  • Turns raw scores into attention weights: all positive, adding up to 1.
  • "What fraction of my attention goes to each token."
  • The same softmax from class 2.3 (Neural networks); bigger scores get exponentially more weight.

Decoded · 4 of 4Blend: weights times values

Multiply the attention weights by the value vectors \( V \) and add them up. Each token's new representation is a weighted blend of every token's value.

The context vectorThe output for "bank" is \( 0.70\,v_{\text{river}} + 0.15\,v_{\text{bank}} + \dots \): mostly river, a little of the rest. The thickest link, and the largest weight, goes to "river", so the word now carries its context.

By the numbersFrom dot products to a blended vector

Score: query dotted with each key
  • Query \( q = (2, 0) \).
  • \( q\cdot k_{\text{river}} = (2,0)\cdot(2,0) = 4 \).
  • \( q\cdot k_{\text{bank}} = (2,0)\cdot(0.5,3) = 1 \).
  • \( q\cdot k_{\text{the}} = (2,0)\cdot(0,1) = 0 \).
  • Scores \( s = (4,\ 1,\ 0) \). (Scaling left out to keep the arithmetic clean.)
Softmax, then blend the values
  • Exponentiate: \( e^4, e^1, e^0 = 54.6,\ 2.72,\ 1.0 \); sum \( 58.3 \).
  • Weights: \( 0.936,\ 0.047,\ 0.017 \), adding to 1.
  • Context \( = 0.936\,v_{\text{river}} + 0.047\,v_{\text{bank}} + 0.017\,v_{\text{the}} \).
The resultAlmost all of "river"'s value, a trace of the rest. That blend is the token's context-aware output.

Self-attentionEvery token, in parallel

When \( Q, K, V \) all come from the same sentence, tokens attend to each other within it. That is self-attention, the layer LLMs are built from.

Why transformers train fastEvery token's attention is computed at the same time, as matrix multiplications, not one step after another. Unlike the RNN from class 3.1 (Text as data), there is no sequential bottleneck.

Self-attentionMasking: no peeking ahead

A model that generates text left to right must not let a token see words that come after it. Causal masking blocks those links before the softmax, so each token attends only to itself and the tokens before it.

Why it mattersThis causal mask is what makes a decoder autoregressive: word \( t \) is predicted from words 1 to \( t-1 \) only, which keeps next-token prediction honest in class 3.5 (From models to LLMs). Masking is a decoder feature. Encoder-only models are bidirectional by design, with no causal mask, so each token sees the whole sentence.

BuildAttention, from scratch, as a heatmap

In the notebook you implement scaled dot-product attention in NumPy on a short sentence, then plot the attention weights as a heatmap. You will see, concretely, which tokens attend to which, for example a pronoun leaning on the noun it refers to.
